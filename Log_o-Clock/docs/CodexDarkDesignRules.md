# Log O'clock — Codex Dark design rules

This interface follows the supplied Windows Codex recreation guide. The values are a system, not isolated decoration.

## Relationships

- Spacing is based on 4 px. Compact gaps are 4–8 px, page gutters 16 px, cards 16–20 px, and dialogs 24 px.
- Surfaces rise from `#181818` (app), through `#212121` (content), to `#282828` (floating/elevated). The sidebar is the intermediate `#1d1d1d` shell tone.
- Interaction is expressed by white overlays: 5% selected/focused, 8% hover, and 12% pressed. Standard controls never retain a blue focus outline; the calendar's blue outline remains reserved for identifying today.
- Border strength follows elevation: 4% separators, 8% fields, and 16% floating-edge rings.
- Radius follows size and elevation: 10 px controls, 12 px navigation rows, 15 px menus, 20 px cards, 22 px timer composer, and 25 px dialogs.
- Type follows hierarchy: 11 px metadata, 12 px menus/navigation, 14 px controls/body, 18–20 px section headings, and 24 px major headings. Use 400/500/600 weights.
- Icons use a 24 px coordinate canvas, 2 px round strokes, 16–20 px visible size, and 28–32 px interaction boxes.
- Motion uses 150 ms for direct feedback, 200 ms for panel opacity, and 300 ms for spatial change. It is suppressed when Windows animations are disabled.
- Long lists, including day-grouped History entries, retain recycling virtualization but scroll in pixels, easing wheel and touchpad input over 150 ms instead of jumping by rows or groups. Nested lists pass scrolling to their parent at the first or last item, and motion becomes immediate when Windows animations are disabled.
- Shadows and translucent surfaces belong only to floating controls. Static panels use surface difference and spacing.

## Layout contract

- Title bar: 46 px.
- The left title-bar identity area ends with a compact neutral profile selector showing the active local workspace and a chevron, followed immediately by the unlabeled full-automatic-mode toggle. Its dark profile menu lists profiles with one checkmark and exposes Add, Rename current, and Remove current actions. The automatic toggle uses the standard gray track while off and semantic success green while on; its tooltip supplies the missing visible label and explains the remembered-boundary grace behavior. Both controls collapse with the sidebar below 1040 px. Profile switching never changes shell color or expands the title bar.
- Modal editors, choosers, color pickers, and alerts reuse the same 46 px custom dark title bar, app icon treatment, border hierarchy, and DWM rounding as the main shell. Transient click-away popovers keep compact popup chrome without a title bar.
- Sidebar: 275 px normally, 240 px in the compact desktop state, floating below 1040 px window width.
- Every persisted target is one individual record shared by Project Settings, the Targets tab, and Reports. Projects may contain multiple targets of the same cadence, and changing cadence preserves the target identity. Each target also persists its duration metric: active time or time including short idle. Project Settings presents its project-scoped targets as a compact list; one dimmed ghost row is shown for each missing Daily, Weekly, and Monthly cadence, but a ghost is not stored until the user assigns hours. The Projects table shows cadence summaries calculated as the sum of individual project targets. The sidebar is deliberately aggregated: one row per active project, plus one global row when needed, with daily/weekly/monthly progress drawn as three overlaid arcs in one ring. The compact sidebar disables horizontal overflow so the ring remains visible and long names trim. The Targets management surface begins with the standard compact project filter: All projects, Global targets, then every active `Project · Client`; entering the tab resets it to All projects. Reports uses a separate scrollable monthly-only view containing every active project-scoped and global monthly target, even when its project has no work in the selected report range. Daily, weekly, and monthly progress uses the same local calendar periods and the target's selected report duration. One-time targets expose no date field or date column, accumulate from creation until reaching their hours, remain present through the Sunday of the completion week, and leave target surfaces at the following Monday boundary.
- Fixed and floating sidebar target rows never add a hover or selected-row surface. They remain hit-testable for editing and context actions, while progress rings, target lettering, debt, and completion fills remain unchanged.
- Reports monthly-target rows never expose hover, pressed, selected, or keyboard-focus decoration. Their transparent row template remains hit-testable so double-click editing and right-click actions work without leaving visual selection state.
- Target context menus expose `Lower debt...` and `Cancel debt` only when the correlated project has positive outstanding debt. Lowering accepts decimal hours or `H:MM`, cannot equal or exceed the current debt, and shows the remaining amount together with the dated reduction. Full cancellation shows its compact amount and local date. The project-target editor groups both as debt adjustments and retains `Bring debt back`; restoration is committed with the rest of the editor and never deletes the adjustment audit records.
- Primary reading/settings column: maximum 768 px. Wide timer/data composition: maximum 896 px.
- Floating controls keep at least 8 px from window edges.
- Data grids retain all columns; narrow screens scroll or switch to an explicitly stacked layout rather than hiding data.
- Reports reserve the wide left column for project and task rows. A neutral `Projects`/`Clients` tab strip switches the primary donut chart in the upper-right summary column without moving the card. Projects reflects the selected report range and active filters; Clients always aggregates all work in the current local calendar month by client. A second project-share donut directly below uses `Time + idle`, which adds back only complete excluded idle intervals at or below the configurable 1–60 minute limit. The aligned task table exposes the same metric immediately after `Time`, followed by `Calls`, Value, and Logs; it does not display separate Paid or Unpaid duration columns. Call time is explicitly marked by the user and remains project-linked through its time entry. Payment filtering and report context actions remain available. Report column visibility and widths form one persisted view: a resize is mirrored across every project task table and its aligned total row, reveals `Save Current View`, and restores on subsequent launches. The summary column scrolls vertically so both charts and current project targets remain reachable, and at narrow widths it stacks below the table as one unit.
- Reports end with the table/chart workspace. Do not add a detached bottom text strip for selected duration, value, or current-month unpaid totals; those values belong in project summary rows, the donut total, columns, and filters.
- The Software tab exposes a project filter and Project/Client columns. `No project` creates one `All projects` row whose tracking, exclusion, and correlated tags are shared globally; selecting a project creates an explicit project-only assignment. Software labels remain global historical display data.
- History and Reports use one compact date-range field. Its dark calendar immediately applies the first click as a single-day range while retaining that day as the anchor for an optional normal second click or native Shift-click range. Every complete inclusive selection is highlighted and applied immediately, the popup stays open until click-away, and the latest selection persists after it closes. The same field accepts normalized `DD.MM.YYYY - DD.MM.YYYY` keyboard input.
- The current calendar day always keeps its blue outline, including when it falls inside the selected range; range fill must not replace the today indicator.
- Report projects use a section header rather than project-level table columns. The project and client share one title line separated by the standard muted dot; task metrics start collapsed behind a neutral chevron, with their aligned column names and a two-pixel project-total footer visible in both states. A subtle one-pixel separator and compact spacing distinguish adjacent project groups without creating card surfaces.
- Removing a tag is a destructive metadata action available from the tag row context menu. Existing descriptions preserve the tag name as ordinary text, while only its exact `#tag` marker and shared color definition are removed.
- Projects, tasks, tags, and window rules use extended row selection. Their shared editor prefills values common to the whole selection, labels differing fields as `Multiple values`, and changes only fields explicitly enabled with an Apply checkbox. Unique project, task, and tag names remain individual.
- Completed entries with less than 60 seconds of net tracked time are discarded automatically across timer stops, manual entry creation, history edits, idle exclusions, recovery, and database upgrades. Exactly 60 seconds remains valid, and discarded entries never reopen the details popup.
- Every History date-group header shows the sum of its currently visible entries in the same fixed column as row durations. The group total uses 14 px Consolas Semibold so it is more prominent than the 12 px entry durations without competing with the date heading. Changing History filters recomputes the daily total from the filtered rows.
- History entry selection is visually passive. Selected rows and cells retain transparent backgrounds and expose no persistent selection indicator; a pointed row still receives the normal neutral hover layer. Extended selection remains functional for multi-entry commands and context menus.
- Filter and chooser options are sourced only from active objects. Removed projects are permanently deleted together with their time entries, tasks, targets, rules, settings, project-scoped links, and debt adjustments; removed targets are permanently deleted too. The schema upgrade purges project/target data that older versions left archived. Task-only removal remains archival so its historical rows retain the saved name and duration.
- Window Rules uses project section headers instead of repeating a project column. Each header displays `Project · Client`, and a compact active-project filter above the list preserves grouped multi-selection, double-click editing, and empty-space or row context menus. Its `Title contains` and `Application` columns split the usable grid width equally on initial display so the Application column is fully visible; narrow screens retain horizontal scrolling rather than hiding a column.
- Plain Enter is the compact commit action for tracking: a completed idle timer bar starts immediately, an active timer bar applies task and description changes in place, and the automatic details popup saves and closes. Open dropdowns retain Enter for option selection, while Shift+Enter remains a description newline.
- Timer-bar dropdown rows live in separate WPF popup visual trees and must still count as part of their owning chooser for click-away handling. Project changes remain enabled while tracking and update the running entry. Task search detaches a previous selected task only after the user changes its text, preserves the new text and caret when opening results, and commits a clicked result to both the editor and running entry.
- The tray differentiates left-click intent using the Windows double-click interval. A confirmed single click reopens the running entry's existing task/description popup (or opens the app when idle); a double click cancels that pending action and opens the main window.
- The tray right-click menu exposes `Cancel current timer` only while tracking. It immediately discards the local running entry without confirmation, while `Stop timer` retains the completed entry and follows the normal details-review flow.
- A running-entry popup opened or reactivated from the tray exposes `Rip from this moment`. Rip saves the popup values into the old entry, ends it and starts its copied successor at one atomic timestamp, then retargets the still-open popup so subsequent edits belong only to the new entry.
- History duration cells show net time first and, when exclusions exist, a gray `− HH:MM:SS idle` line beneath it. The subtraction uses 8 px type beneath a 12 px duration and remains vertically centered in a 42 px row. Completed-entry editing exposes the same aggregate exclusion as an editable `HH:MM:SS` value and preserves the one-minute minimum.

- Accumulated short-idle review uses a rolling four-hour window and is independent from excluded-software review. Only detected idle intervals strictly below five minutes qualify; they retain their originating entry across timer changes, while portions older than four hours expire automatically. Accepting cuts the retained intervals from their original logs and resets the reminder to 1x. Declining retains them and advances the next reminder from 1x directly to 3x, then to each following whole multiple. Long idle, unavailable-session, and excluded-software intervals never enter this total. Long-idle reviews are serialized: activity monitoring remains live while a review is visible, each later qualifying interval keeps its own timestamps, and every interval that began during an open review is shown afterward with the `Double trouble!` heading. Only one Keep/Cut dialog may be visible at a time.
- Settings groups idle protection immediately after Windows session behavior. The two switches use the shared neutral toggle style, explanations use secondary text, and one compact status surface reports every active reason. Active reasons share the semantic success color; inactive, unavailable, and initializing states remain neutral. The visible privacy line must always state that detection is output-only and microphone activity is never accessed.

## Product color contract

Project and tag colors remain user data and may appear in dots, labels, charts, and tag runs. They must not recolor shell selection or standard buttons. Primary actions are white/black; ordinary interaction and keyboard focus are neutral gray; the calendar alone uses blue to identify today; running/success is green; destructive/error is red.

The Software editor's exclusion switch uses the same checked palette as Settings switches: `ContentPrimaryBrush` for the track and app `#181818` for the thumb. Its custom template keeps the focus visual suppressed and does not alter the hide/show behavior of correlated tags.
